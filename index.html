How Zecrow works

Protection you can follow.

Six ideas carry every transaction — from first payment to final resolution. Hover or focus a card to open it.

The problem

Trade breaks where trust breaks.

Online commerce runs on whoever moves first — and hopes the other side follows.

Buyers pay upfront hoping goods arrive; sellers deliver hoping payment is secure. Traditional escrow adds another intermediary, more fees, and one more party both sides must trust.

Shared control

No single key moves the money.

Buyer, seller, and arbitrator each hold a key — any two together authorize a payout.

The proposed 2-of-3 multisignature arrangement means no participant can act alone. Agreement pays the seller; dispute brings the arbitrator into a signed resolution.

Rules in code

Cryptography enforces the agreement.

Amount, delivery terms, and release conditions are set before money moves.

Signatures authorize each outcome under a visible 2-of-3 rule. Nothing releases automatically and no smart contract stands behind it — every payout follows the agreed rules through real signatures.

AI evidence review

Disputes get examined, not ignored.

An AI pipeline built for one job: turning messy dispute evidence into a review the arbitrator can act on.

Every case runs four passes. Collect gathers the agreement, messages, delivery records, files, and tracking. Compare checks each item against the original terms and both sides’ claims. Identify flags what conflicts, what is missing, and which requirements look unmet. Summarize reports what the evidence supports, what stays uncertain, and what needs a human — a logo package with all three formats present but one requirement contested, for example. The boundaries are hard: the AI holds no keys, authorizes no payouts, invents no proof, and never decides. The arbitrator decides.

Intended design
Digital goods

Delivered online, judged by the terms.

Files and freelance work deliver instantly — acceptance still follows the agreement.

The seller submits the deliverable; the buyer confirms or disputes. Upload alone releases nothing, and no automatic check of file quality is claimed — the evidence decides.

Physical goods

Shipped in the real world, tracked end to end.

Physical listings carry AI-checked descriptions from the very start.

The seller uploads photos and a full description, and the AI reviews them for strength and consistency before a payment link can even be created. The buyer then approves the complete description before paying — both sides agree on exactly what is being bought, with the evidence bar set before money moves. Proof of arrival still needs real evidence, never the signature mechanism alone.

Release code

One code releases the funds.

The buyer confirms receipt with a one-time delivery code — a valid code triggers the normal release.
