How Zecrow works

Protection you can follow.

Six ideas carry every transaction, from first payment to final resolution. Hover or focus a card to open it.

The problem

Trade breaks where trust breaks.

Online commerce runs on whoever moves first, hoping the other side follows.

Buyers pay upfront and hope goods arrive. Sellers deliver and hope payment is secure. Traditional escrow inserts another intermediary with its own fees, delays, and custody, asking both sides to trust one more stranger.

Shared control

No single key moves the money.

Buyer, seller, and arbitrator each hold a key, and any two together authorize a payout.

That 2-of-3 rule is the whole trick. Buyer plus seller releases payment. Buyer plus arbitrator, or seller plus arbitrator, resolves a dispute. No single key can move funds alone, not even the arbitrator's.

Rules in code

Cryptography enforces the agreement.

Amount, delivery terms, and release conditions are set before money moves.

Terms are set first, then every outcome is authorized under a visible 2-of-3 rule signed by real keys. Nothing releases automatically and no smart contract stands behind it. Code is the rulebook here, and signatures are the only hand that executes it.

AI evidence review

Disputes get examined, not ignored.

An AI pipeline built for one job: turning messy dispute evidence into a review the arbitrator can act on.

Every case runs four passes. Collect gathers the agreement, messages, delivery records, files, and tracking. Compare checks each item against the original terms and both sides’ claims. Identify flags what conflicts, what is missing, and which requirements look unmet. Summarize reports what the evidence supports, what stays uncertain, and what needs a human, like a logo package with all three formats present but one requirement contested. The boundaries are hard: the AI holds no keys, authorizes no payouts, invents no proof, and never decides. The arbitrator decides.

Intended design
Digital goods

Delivered online, judged by the terms.

Files and freelance work deliver instantly, but acceptance still follows the agreement.

The seller submits the deliverable, and the AI compares it against the acceptance criteria, flagging format gaps, missing pieces, and mismatches for the record. The buyer confirms or disputes. Upload alone releases nothing, and no automatic verdict on quality is ever claimed.

Physical goods

Shipped in the real world, tracked end to end.

Physical listings carry AI-checked descriptions from the very start.

The seller uploads photos and a full description, and the AI reviews them for strength and consistency before a payment link can even be created. The buyer then approves the complete description before paying, so both sides agree on exactly what is being bought, with the evidence bar set before money moves. Proof of arrival still needs real evidence, never the signature mechanism alone.
